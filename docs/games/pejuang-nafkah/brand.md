## 1. The line

### WNI Series (umbrella)

**“Susahnya nyata, dramanya gratis.”**

Lockup variant for merchandise/social: **“WNI: susahnya nyata, dramanya gratis.”**

The longer, explanatory line:

**“Jadi WNI itu susah. Untung di game bisa di-pause.”**

Pure-Indonesian variant if native review prefers it: **“Jadi WNI itu susah. Untung di game bisa dijeda.”**

The short line has three jobs: it admits the struggle (**susahnya nyata**), it names the thing people actually retell (**dramanya**), and it makes the game's promise (**gratis** — the drama costs you nothing here, unlike the real morning). It is self-deprecating, not cynical about the audience.

### Pejuang Nafkah

**“Berangkat. Berjuang. Sampai. Gitu aja terus setiap hari.”**

Supporting lines:

- **“Bukan balapan. Ini perjalanan menuju gajian.”**
- **“Satu jempol. Satu tujuan: gajian.”**
- **“Nafkahnya nyata, dramanya gratis.”**

The first three beats are the heroic commute. The last line is the truth said with a shrug: it repeats, and somehow we keep going. It should feel like a tired laugh shared with someone else on the platform — not despair, not a motivational poster.

### Why “dramanya”, not “lucunya”

“Lucunya gratis” was a good line, but it described the **tone**. Drama is the **content**: the flood, the demo, the small accident, the queue fight, the day the train stopped. The game is funny because the character meets drama with **bercanda dan santai**, not because the drama itself is a joke. “Dramanya gratis” promises the memorable part without charging for it — and without pretending the real city is a theme park.

## 2. Drama is the second engine

Drama is a **core characteristic and principle**, not an occasional event. It is how the game stays fresh: the player should open a run wondering, _“Wah, ada drama apa hari ini?”_

The brand rules for drama:

1. **Real basis, fictionalised detail.** Every drama family must be documented as something that happened or still recurs in the city. The specific card is a composite, not a reconstruction. No invented flood on a street that never floods, no fake demo, no fake accident.
2. **Rare and irregular.** Major drama is not every run. It arrives when conditions and the seed allow it. Small absurdities can be common; a full-blown drama should feel like a day, not a slot machine.
3. **Fair.** The player can read it, choose a response, and find a way through. Drama costs time, money, or dignity; it does not make the run unwinnable.
4. **Bercanda dulu, panik belakangan.** The character names the absurdity, makes a small joke, then does the practical thing. The joke is a breath, not denial.
5. **Never at a real victim's expense.** No real recent tragedy, named victim, graphic injury, communal blame, or disaster tourism. A serious beat gets warmth, not a punchline.
6. **Made to be retold.** A drama should produce a line the player wants to send to a friend: “Hari ini banjir, demo, dan motorku mati. Sampai juga.”

**Drama copy examples (direction, not final cards):**

| Situation                    | Bang Nafkah's angle                                 |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Underpass floods             | “Kolam renang gratis. Sayangnya bukan hari libur.”  |
| Demo closes the road         | “Jalan ditutup. Pendapat boleh. Kita cari putaran.” |
| Minor accident ahead         | “Semua pelan. Semua telat. Semua pulang.”           |
| Queue fight                  | “Ributnya lima menit. Basahnya seharian.”           |
| Train stops between stations | “Kereta mikir dulu. Kita ikut mikir.”               |
| Angkot stops anywhere        | “Berhenti di mana saja, sampai di mana-mana.”       |

**Do not:** make a tragedy the twist, use a real disaster photo, turn a victim into a lesson, make an identity group the cause, or use “drama” as an excuse for cruelty. If the honest response is silence, be silent.

The template is deliberately portable across WNI Series games. The short series line stays fixed; the `[X]` changes. Do not force the template onto a subject too sensitive for wordplay (e.g., a real disaster with victims); use the warm-but-serious register instead.

## 5. Bang Nafkah

**Who he is:** the narrator and brand face of Pejuang Nafkah. Not the player character; the voice in the player's head. A commuter in his 30s with helmet hair, a thermos, a cheap rain jacket, and the calm of someone who has missed the same train before.

**What he does:** notices the absurd detail, names the drama without escalating it, says the quiet part, then helps the player find the next move. He is funny because he is observant and unbothered, not because he is cruel. He is the friend who says “ya sudah, kita cari jalan lain” while everyone else is still arguing.

**Catchphrases (use sparingly):**

- “Gitu aja terus setiap hari.”
- “Bercanda dulu, panik belakangan.”
- “Drama boleh. Panik jangan.”
- “Gajian dulu.”
- “Yang penting sampai.”
- “Kursi kosong itu mitos.”
- “Ini bukan macet, ini meditasi berjamaah.”
- “Tenang. Paniknya nanti setelah sampai.”

**Visual:** cream sticker, thick ink outline, teal helmet, amber thermos, tired eyes, small smile. A sticker, not a mascot costume. He can appear on the landing page, loading screens, drama cards, report cards, and social cards. **Original character:** no real-person/celebrity likeness; no official operator uniform or logo.

**Do not:** make him a know-it-all, a lecturer, a pick-up artist, or a punching-down uncle. He never jokes about a real victim, a disability, a woman's body, poverty, religion, or ethnicity. He does not tell the player to calm down when the player is genuinely angry; he makes room for the anger first.

## 6. Voice rules

1. **Warm, dry, specific.** A joke works because it names the exact thing: the angkot that stops anywhere, the door that closes in eight seconds, the rain that only makes the commuter wet.
2. **Punchline, then warmth.** End with a small human beat, not a sneer. Example: “Kursi kosong itu mitos. Tapi kalau dapat, jangan lupa bersyukur.”
3. **One joke per beat.** Do not stack jokes until the emotion disappears. A serious failure gets silence, a breath, or a kind line — not a quip.
4. **Laugh at the situation, the system, and yourself.** Never at the poor, the disabled, women, minorities, or real victims.
5. **No “ngakak”, no meme slogans, no dated slang.** The game should still read in five years.
6. **Kamu, not Anda.** Everyday Indonesian. No corporate voice. No fake street slang.
7. **The character is funny; the game does not force the player to be funny.** The player can be tired, quiet, or angry. Humour is an invitation, not a demand.
8. **Never use humour to hide a paywall, a price, or a bug.** Honesty first; the joke comes after.
9. **Drama is met with bercanda dan santai, not denial.** The character acknowledges the problem, makes it bearable, then acts. “Santai” is not “tidak peduli”.
10. **The player's real hardship is not content.** We fictionalise the city, not the player's grief. If a card would make someone relive a real loss, cut it.

## 8. Native and sensitivity review

- Test the lines with 8–12 Jabodetabek commuters; if a line feels like it punches down, cut it.
- Every drama family gets a **sensitivity review** against [ADR-0005](../../adr/0005-drama-system.md): real basis, no real victims, no recent tragedy, no identity blame, no graphic detail.
- The funniest line should never be the only line in a serious moment.
- Keep a **joke ledger** and a **drama ledger** in the content pack with source/author/reviewer and the context where each is allowed.
- The English localization can be drier and more literal; do not try to translate every pun. “Susahnya nyata, dramanya gratis” → “The struggle is real. The drama is free.”
