> **Rule:** the joke is a coping mechanism, not a verdict. We laugh at the situation, the system, and ourselves — never at people who are already carrying the day.

---

## 1. The line

### WNI Series (umbrella)

**“Susahnya nyata. Lucunya gratis.”**

Lockup variant for merchandise/social: **“WNI: susahnya nyata, lucunya gratis.”**

The longer, explanatory line:

**“Jadi WNI itu susah. Untung di game bisa di-pause.”**

Pure-Indonesian variant if native review prefers it: **“Jadi WNI itu susah. Untung di game bisa dijeda.”**

Use the short line on the website, OG image, footer, and press kit. Use the long line when there is room to explain the promise. Both are self-deprecating, not cynical about the audience.

### Pejuang Nafkah

**“Berangkat. Berjuang. Sampai. Besok lagi.”**

Supporting lines:

- **“Bukan balapan. Ini perjalanan menuju gajian.”**
- **“Satu jempol. Satu tujuan: gajian.”**
- **“Nafkahnya nyata. Lucunya gratis.”**

The four-beat line is the main game slogan. The first three beats are the heroic commute; **“Besok lagi.”** is the punchline that tells the truth: it repeats. It should feel like a tired laugh, not despair.

### Expansion template

`[X]nya nyata. Lucunya gratis.`

| Game           | Line                                  |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Pejuang Nafkah | **Nafkahnya nyata. Lucunya gratis.**  |
| Pulang (mudik) | **Mudiknya nyata. Lucunya gratis.**   |
| Warung         | **Dagangnya nyata. Lucunya gratis.**  |
| Kampung        | **Kampungnya nyata. Lucunya gratis.** |

The template is deliberately portable across WNI Series games. The short series line stays fixed; the `[X]` changes. Do not force the template onto a game whose subject is too sensitive for wordplay (e.g., a real disaster); use the warm-but-serious register instead.

## 3. Bang Nafkah

**Who he is:** the narrator and brand face of Pejuang Nafkah. Not the player character; the voice in the player’s head. A commuter in his 30s with helmet hair, a thermos, a cheap rain jacket, and the calm of someone who has missed the same train before.

**What he does:** notices the absurd detail, says the quiet part, then makes the player feel less alone. He is funny because he is observant, not because he is cruel.

**Catchphrases (use sparingly):**

- “Besok lagi.”
- “Gajian dulu.”
- “Yang penting sampai.”
- “Kursi kosong itu mitos.”
- “Ini bukan macet, ini meditasi berjamaah.”
- “Tenang. Paniknya nanti setelah sampai.”

**Visual:** cream sticker, thick ink outline, teal helmet, amber thermos, tired eyes, small smile. A sticker, not a mascot costume. He can appear on the landing page, loading screens, report cards, and social cards. **Original character:** no real-person/celebrity likeness; no official operator uniform or logo.

**Do not:** make him a know-it-all, a lecturer, a pick-up artist, or a punching-down uncle. He never jokes about a real victim, a disability, a woman’s body, poverty, religion, or ethnicity.

## 4. Voice rules

1. **Warm, dry, specific.** A joke works because it names the exact thing: the angkot that stops anywhere, the door that closes in eight seconds, the rain that only makes the commuter wet.
2. **Punchline, then warmth.** End with a small human beat, not a sneer. Example: “Kursi kosong itu mitos. Tapi kalau dapat, jangan lupa bersyukur.”
3. **One joke per beat.** Do not stack jokes until the emotion disappears. A serious failure gets silence, a breath, or a kind line — not a quip.
4. **Laugh at the situation, the system, and yourself.** Never at the poor, the disabled, women, minorities, or real victims.
5. **No “ngakak”, no meme slogans, no dated slang.** The game should still read in five years.
6. **Kamu, not Anda.** Everyday Indonesian. No corporate voice. No fake street slang.
7. **The character is funny; the game does not force the player to be funny.** The player can be tired, quiet, or angry. Humour is an invitation, not a demand.
8. **Never use humour to hide a paywall, a price, or a bug.** Honesty first; the joke comes after.

## 6. Native review notes

- Test the lines with 8–12 Jabodetabek commuters; if a line feels like it punches down, cut it.
- The funniest line should never be the only line in a serious moment.
- Keep a “joke ledger” in the content pack with source/author/reviewer and the context where it is allowed.
- The English localization can be drier and more literal; do not try to translate every pun.
